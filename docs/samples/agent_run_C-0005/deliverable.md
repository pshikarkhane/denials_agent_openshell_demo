APPEAL — the clinical record substantiates all three medical-necessity criteria in policy MP-IMG-007, so the denial should be overturned.

## Reasoning

Policy MP-IMG-007 (effective 2026-04-15, applies to this date of service) requires three criteria for lumbar spine MRI medical necessity:

**Section 2.1(a) — at least 6 weeks of conservative therapy.** The primary care visit note of 2026-03-23 documents that conservative management was begun (NSAIDs, home exercise program, physical therapy referral) (source: notes/2026-03-23_pcp.txt). The physical therapy attendance log documents 21 PT visits from 2026-03-23 through 2026-06-04, a treatment period of 73 days (approximately 10 weeks) (source: supporting/pt_log.txt). The orthopedic consultation note of 2026-06-04 confirms conservative management failed to provide adequate relief and that an MRI was ordered (source: notes/2026-06-04_ortho.txt). Status: met.

**Section 2.1(b) — a neurological deficit or recognized red flag finding.** The orthopedic consultation note of 2026-06-04 documents a positive straight leg raise at 40 degrees, left leg paresthesias, and mildly reduced left ankle dorsiflexion strength (source: notes/2026-06-04_ortho.txt). These are documented neurological deficits. Status: met.

**Section 2.1(c) — no prior lumbar imaging within the preceding 12 months.** The orthopedic consultation note of 2026-06-04 states: "Imaging history: No prior lumbar imaging on file." (source: notes/2026-06-04_ortho.txt). Status: met.

The denial stated that the submitted clinical information did not demonstrate medical necessity, but the record on file establishes all three criteria. The claim was timely filed: the payer received it on 2026-06-26 (source: denial_letter.txt), and policy MP-IMG-007 section 7.2 sets a 180-day timely-filing limit (source: MP-IMG-007.txt). No corrected claim is needed because the claim data are not defective — the issue is medical necessity documentation, which the record supports.
